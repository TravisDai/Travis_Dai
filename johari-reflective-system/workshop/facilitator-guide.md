# Facilitator guide

## Core message

The system is a **structured reflective interaction**, not a diagnostic instrument.

A useful facilitator phrase is:

> “The four labels tell the AI what kind of contribution to make; they do not tell us what reflective state the educator is in.”

## Before the session

- Confirm which AI service participants may use.
- Check institutional guidance on uploading teaching material.
- Ask participants to prepare de-identified examples.
- Have the reference prompt and weekly log available.
- Decide whether participants will work individually or in pairs.

## During the session

### Keep evidence and interpretation separate

When the AI produces a claim, ask:
- Where in the supplied material does that come from?
- Is the AI summarising, interpreting, or proposing?

### Normalise rejection

A good outcome is not “accepting more AI suggestions”. Participants should reject or modify suggestions that are not contextually sound.

### Keep Blind non-diagnostic

Blind is a critique/alternative-interpretation function. Do not present AI output as proof of a psychological blind spot.

### Keep Unknown bounded

Ask for plausible, context-sensitive options and observable review indicators. Novelty alone is not value.

## Questions for group discussion

- Which section mostly repeated what you already knew?
- Which alternative interpretation was worth checking?
- Which question helped make an assumption explicit?
- Which idea was feasible enough to consider?
- Where did the AI lack context?
- What would you need to record if you wanted to evaluate this process rigorously later?

## For research-oriented workshops

If you intend to study workshop outcomes:
- design the measurement separately from the four prompt labels;
- use a pre-specified coding/evaluation scheme;
- create persistent suggestion-to-action IDs if later uptake matters;
- preserve missing values rather than treating missing as zero;
- document prompt and model provenance prospectively.
