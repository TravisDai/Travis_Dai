# 60-minute workshop: Using a Johari-inspired GenAI routine for educator reflection

## Intended outcome

Participants leave with:
- experience of the four interaction functions;
- one critically reviewed reflective output;
- one educator-authored note or candidate action; and
- a clear understanding of the difference between AI orchestration and evaluation.

## 0–8 min — introduction

Introduce:
- the Johari Window as conceptual inspiration;
- Open = consolidation;
- Blind = critique;
- Hidden = assumption elicitation;
- Unknown = ideation;
- human review as a required part of the workflow.

Make clear that the system is not a diagnostic or self-awareness scale.

## 8–15 min — prepare a teaching case

Each participant selects a small current teaching issue.

Ask participants to remove identifying student information before using an AI service.

Suggested inputs:
- one lesson/lab/assessment excerpt;
- one common difficulty;
- the intended learning outcome;
- a short note about context and constraints.

## 15–30 min — run the four-function prompt

Participants use the [reference weekly prompt](../prompts/reference-weekly-prompt.md).

Encourage them to mark:
- evidence;
- interpretation;
- useful questions;
- candidate actions.

## 30–40 min — educator review

Participants choose two AI outputs and classify their own response:

- accept;
- modify;
- reject;
- unresolved.

Ask them to explain why.

## 40–50 min — peer discussion

In pairs or small groups discuss:
- Which function was most useful?
- Where did the AI overreach?
- Which assumptions became more explicit?
- Which idea, if any, is worth considering?
- What evidence would be needed to judge a later trial?

Do not compare “scores” across the four labels.

## 50–57 min — make a traceable note

Participants complete the [weekly reflection log](../templates/weekly-reflection-log.md).

If a candidate action may be tried later, give it a persistent suggestion ID.

## 57–60 min — debrief

Reinforce:
- prompting structure is not measurement;
- AI output is not evidence of effectiveness;
- good provenance makes later evaluation possible;
- educator judgement remains central.

## Optional follow-up

At a later meeting, participants can bring back their selected action IDs and discuss whether each action was trialled, modified, rejected, deferred, or not evaluable.
